One line has been everywhere in my feed since Sunday night. Yash Patil, co-founder and CEO of Applied Compute, posted: "If you're bullish on AI, you should believe services businesses can scale like software."

It landed with weight because of the week he's had. On November 18, Palo Alto Networks agreed to acquire Applied Compute in an all-stock deal valued at $4.5 billion, subject to adjustments and regulatory clearance. Patil is set to become Palo Alto's Co-Chief AI Officer after the close. To be fair about the context: Palo Alto's own announcement frames the deal around open-weight AI and Applied Compute's research team, not around services firms. But the post struck a nerve with people who run consulting, accounting, legal and agency businesses, and it's worth taking seriously.

## What changed

Services businesses have always grown in a straight line. More clients meant more people. Margins stayed roughly where they were, because every new dollar of revenue came with a new slice of payroll attached.

Software grows differently. You build it once and the next customer costs very little to serve.

What AI changes is the middle. A lot of service work is research, drafting, data entry, follow-up, reconciliation and status reporting. That's the part agents are now getting good at. When that part stops needing a person every time, a services firm's cost to serve the next client starts to look less like headcount and more like infrastructure.

## Why it matters for operators

Here's the catch most firms will hit. You can't scale work that only exists in people's heads.

If every engagement is run a little differently, if the real process lives in one senior person's inbox, and if client data is spread across spreadsheets and email threads, there's nothing for an agent to repeat. You'll get a faster version of the same chaos.

The firms that pull this off will do the boring part first:

- **Write the process down.** Pick the work you do for every client and define the steps, the inputs and what "done" looks like.
- **Put the data in one system.** Agents need a source of truth with clear permissions, not ten tabs and a shared drive.
- **Automate the repeatable middle.** Intake, research, drafts, reminders and reporting go to the agents. Judgment, relationships and final sign-off stay with people.
- **Price the outcome, not the hours.** If the work gets faster and you still bill by the hour, you've automated your own revenue away.

## My take

I've watched this happen inside our own company. Infraxio is seven people today, down from a team of 30 before AI. We deploy a full business hub in under three weeks, where it used to take four to six months. That didn't come from one clever tool. It came from turning how we work into systems that run the same way every time, and then letting AI do the repeatable parts.

That's also most of what we build for clients. IFX Hub gives a business one place for its process and data. Growth7, IFX Bid and the custom systems we deploy take the repeated work off people's plates and leave the decisions with them.

So I agree with Patil. Services can scale like software. But only the firms that are willing to build their services like software will get there.
