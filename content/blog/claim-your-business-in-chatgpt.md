On November 10, OpenAI launched [Businesses in ChatGPT](https://openai.com/index/introducing-businesses-chatgpt) in beta. Through a new Business Hub, a company can claim its listing in ChatGPT, verify it, update key details, and upload product feeds with pricing and availability. Business Hub also shows aggregated insights: how often the business is mentioned, website clicks, and the "discovery topics" where its site gets cited.

The second piece is bigger. Eligible U.S. ecommerce and online service businesses with a website can now build a Business Agent, a clearly labeled agent that answers customer questions using information the business provides. When your company shows up in a ChatGPT answer, a customer can click through to your profile and start a conversation with your agent. Businesses can also sponsor that agent and use it as the landing spot for ChatGPT ads. Shopify merchants can connect their store to speed up verification.

OpenAI says it doesn't share individual user conversations or Business Agent chats with the business; insights are aggregated and only appear above certain thresholds. It puts ChatGPT's audience at 1.2 billion people a week. [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/10/openai-rolls-out-new-business-profiles-making-chatgpt-just-a-little-bit-more-like-google/) compared it to the profiles Google has offered for years, and that's the right frame. [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/5f374b32b07b/technology/openai-lets-businesses-claim-chatgpt-profiles-adds-ai-agents-us-e-commerce-firms-2026-11-10/) and [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1030552/openai-chatgpt-business-agents-hub) covered the rollout, and the story was all over my X feed yesterday.

## Why operators should care

For years, the place a customer first met your business was a search result you didn't control. Now a growing share of those first meetings happen inside an AI answer, and until this week you had no official way to correct what it said about you.

Claiming the listing is a ten-minute job. Do it. But the listing isn't what decides whether this helps you.

What decides it is whether the information you feed in is true today. A Business Agent answers from your product details, your return policy, your pricing, and your instructions. If your return policy lives in three places and two of them are out of date, the agent will confidently give a customer the wrong one. If your inventory feed lags your warehouse by a day, the agent will sell something you don't have. OpenAI's own help article walks merchants through testing the agent with a common question, like whether a sale item can be returned, and fixing it if the answer is wrong. That test is only as good as the source data.

## My take

This is the same problem we see in every business we build systems for at Infraxio. The AI layer is never the hard part. The hard part is having one place where the real answer lives: current prices, real availability, the actual policy, the person who owns the exception.

Most small and mid-size companies don't have that yet. They have a website someone updates when they remember, a spreadsheet, and a few people who just know. That worked when a human answered the phone. It does not work when an agent answers thousands of questions a day on your behalf, in a place you don't own.

So before you build a Business Agent, I'd do three things:

1. **Claim and verify the listing now**, so nobody else defines you there first.
2. **Pick one source of truth** for products, pricing, hours, and policies, and make your website, your feeds, and your agent all read from it. That's the kind of operating hub we build with IFX Hub.
3. **Watch the discovery topics.** They tell you what people are actually asking about you. That's free market research, and it's a good brief for the content and campaigns we run through Growth7.

Then build the agent, test it with your ten most common questions, and assign a person to review what it says every week.

Being listed in ChatGPT is about to become table stakes. Being right there is what will separate the companies customers trust.
