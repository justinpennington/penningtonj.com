People ask how I run three companies at once. The honest answer is that I do not run them the way you might run one. Each company has its own team, its own rhythm, and its own operating system. My job is strategy, architecture, and the connective tissue between them.

## Lesson One: Systems Scale, Heroics Do Not

When you run one company, you can compensate for bad systems with personal effort. You stay late, you fix the spreadsheet, you remember the thing no one wrote down. When you run three, that approach collapses immediately. Every company needs systems that work without you in the loop for daily operations.

## Lesson Two: Overlap Is a Feature

Infraxio does technology consulting. Growth7 does growth marketing. DockOps does logistics software. They sound unrelated, but the overlap is real. An Infraxio client who needs marketing gets introduced to Growth7. A logistics client who needs yard management gets introduced to DockOps. The companies reinforce each other.

## Lesson Three: Focus Time Is Sacred

The hardest part of running multiple companies is protecting focus time. Context switching between three different businesses in one day is destructive. I block time by company: Monday and Tuesday are Infraxio days, Wednesday is Growth7, Thursday is DockOps, Friday is cross-company strategy and admin. That structure is not rigid, but it gives each company dedicated attention.

## Lesson Four: Hire for Ownership

You cannot run three companies if every decision flows through you. Each company needs people who own their area, make decisions within it, and only escalate what genuinely requires my input. Hiring for ownership is different from hiring for skill — it requires judgment, initiative, and comfort with ambiguity.
