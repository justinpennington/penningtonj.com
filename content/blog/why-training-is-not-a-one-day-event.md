---
title: "Why Training Is Not a One-Day Event"
date: "2026-07-06"
excerpt: "A four-hour training session the week before go-live is not training. It is a checkbox. Real training is continuous and contextual."
---

The standard implementation playbook allocates a day or two for end-user training right before go-live. By then, the team is overwhelmed, the system is unfamiliar, and the old way of doing things is still fresh. Retention is terrible. Within a week, people are asking each other how to do basic tasks because no one remembers the training.

## Training During Configuration

The better approach is to involve end users during the configuration phase. When you are setting up a workflow, bring in the person who will use it. Let them click through it, make mistakes, and ask questions while there is still time to change things. By go-live, the workflow is not new to them — they helped shape it.

## Micro-Training Over Marathon Sessions

Instead of a full-day session, break training into fifteen-minute modules tied to specific tasks. How to create a sales order. How to process a return. How to run the daily inventory report. Each module is short enough to digest and specific enough to be immediately useful.

## The Thirty-Day Follow-Up

The critical training period is not the week before go-live; it is the thirty days after. That is when users encounter the edge cases, the exceptions, and the workflows they were not trained on because they happen once a month. Having a support plan for that period — whether it is an internal champion, an on-call consultant, or a help-desk channel — is what separates a successful launch from a rocky one.

## Measure Adoption, Not Attendance

Training attendance is a vanity metric. What matters is adoption: are people using the system correctly? Are they using the workflows as designed, or are they inventing workarounds? Tracking adoption in the first sixty days tells you exactly where additional training is needed.
