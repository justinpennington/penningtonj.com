Every ERP vendor will tell you their platform is the one that finally makes implementation painless. None of them will tell you that the biggest risk sits in the room during the kickoff meeting: misaligned expectations between the people buying the system and the people who will use it every day.

## The Gap Between Purchase and Adoption

When a company decides to buy an ERP, the decision is usually made by leadership. They have seen the demos, they understand the ROI projections, and they sign the contract. But the warehouse manager, the AP clerk, and the sales coordinator who will live inside this system eight hours a day were often not in those meetings. By the time they see the new interface, the old way of doing things has already been declared dead, and the replacement feels like a punishment rather than an upgrade.

## Defining Success Before Selecting Software

At Infraxio, we start every engagement by asking a deceptively simple question: what does your business need this system to actually do, expressed in workflows rather than feature checklists? The answer is rarely what the vendor brochure covers. A distributor might need a pick-pack-ship flow that accommodates last-minute order changes without re-keying data. A services firm might need time tracking that feeds directly into invoicing without a manual export step. Those real workflows become the acceptance criteria, not the feature matrix.

## Ownership Over Outsourcing

The other pattern I see in failed implementations is the assumption that the integrator will handle everything. That mindset turns your team into passengers. The companies that get the most from a new system are the ones who designate an internal champion — someone who understands both the business process and the technology well enough to push back on bad configuration decisions and pull their colleagues forward into the new way of working.

## The Takeaway

If you are about to start an ERP project, spend the first two weeks on process mapping and stakeholder alignment, not software configuration. The configuration will go faster and stick longer when everyone agrees on what the system is supposed to do before a single field is customized.
