The maintenance agreement is in a folder. The reorder is "when we run low," which means when someone happens to look. The warranty on the equipment you installed last spring is a line on an invoice PDF. The contract you already won has an option year, and the folder is still named for the first solicitation. Someone meant to call. The week filled up.

That work was already sold. The second time should have been the easier sale. It leaves quietly, because nothing in the system owned the next time.

Ask which accounts are due this month, and who reaches them when the usual person is on a job. The answer is a spreadsheet, a calendar, or whoever closed the last one. That person is the system.

## A Reminder Is Not a Workflow

Most of these businesses already have the date. It sits on a calendar, in a column, or in the head of whoever closed the last job. A date does not do the work.

A calendar reminder belongs to one login. It does not know the last scope, the contact who actually answers, or who covers when that person is out. It fires once. Dismiss it on a busy Tuesday and the next sale waits until someone remembers by accident.

A spreadsheet column is a list you have to go visit. It does not create a task, and it does not notice that the date passed with no touch. On the week the board is full, nobody opens it. The column still looks complete.

This is the handoff after done. I wrote about [where revenue leaks between systems](/blog/the-handoff-is-where-revenue-leaks). The job closed, the invoice went out, the CRM says won. The leak is the gap between this job and the next one, and that gap has no record.

## The Tools Stop at the First Sale

CRM and ERP software is drawn around the new deal. A lead arrives, a stage moves, a quote goes out, a job is won, a status says closed. That path gets the screens, because acquisition is where the budget goes.

The second sale barely exists in the model. Closed is the end of the record, not the start of the next one. A service interval is a custom field nobody's queue reads. A reorder point is a report someone was going to build after go-live. A warranty end is a date. A maintenance contract is a PDF in the job folder. The system can tell you what you sold. It cannot tell you what you are supposed to sell again, to whom, how early, and who has to move.

Storing the customer is not the same as working the account. I wrote about [treating the CRM as a database](/blog/crm-is-not-a-database). A contact with a renewal date and no next step is still a contact list. The work never appears on anyone's day.

Retention gets a slide. Acquisition gets the campaign and the headcount. The second sale is an operations problem. It happens only if the close of one job creates the start of the next. Memory does not cover a sick day.

## Every Close Creates the Next Event

Recurrence belongs in the workflow. When a job or a contract is marked done, that record creates its own next event: a renewal date, a service interval, a reorder point, a warranty end, a re-bid. An event the close produces, the way a won quote should produce a job.

Three things have to travel with it.

**The next date, from the record.** It comes from the job, the contract, or the order that just finished, with the last scope still attached. A separate list drifts the first month nobody updates it.

**An owner and a lead time.** A named person, not the team. The task shows up before the contract lapses and before the customer has called someone else. A date that appears the morning it is due is a surprise. If the owner is out, the work needs a cover, the same as any other task on the board.

**A path for the week nobody acts.** The email bounces. The customer does not answer. The scope changed. The date passes. That case needs a state and a next step. If the only behavior is silence, the account left and the system still looks current.

Miss any of the three and you are back to a reminder. The record closed. The next sale is optional again.

Option years, recurring requirements, and the date you have to re-bid sit on the contract, with an owner and enough lead time to decide whether you are pursuing again. A re-bid that starts because someone noticed an old folder is a contract you already won, handed back to chance.

When that pattern lives in a scheduler's head, a missed week shows up as a yard problem, and a change this week can erase next week.

On a custom build we ask it before drawing the close button. When this job, contract, or shipment is marked done, what next event does the record create, who owns it, how early does it show up, and what state does it take if nobody moves it?

## An Agent Can Draft the Note

A renewal report is easy to commission and easy to ignore. It lists dates. It does not hand anyone a task. I have written about [why nobody opens the dashboard](/blog/nobody-opens-the-dashboard). A screen you have to remember to visit is the spreadsheet with a login. The renewal has to arrive as work: an owner, a due date, and a state when it stalls.

An agent fits in a narrow part of that. It can draft the outreach from the last job, and flag the account that is coming due or went quiet. That saves a pass through the list. It is not the relationship.

The person who knows the customer still owns the ask. They know whether this one is a straight repeat, a scope conversation, or a pass. The agent drafts and flags. A person sends, or decides not to, and the decision stays on the record. If the model is the only thing that remembered, you moved the calendar into a chat window.

## What to Set Up This Week

You can see the gap without a new platform. Take the repeat work you already do and write the workflow on one page.

**Name the kinds of next time you actually have.** Renewal, service interval, reorder, warranty end, re-bid, standing appointment. If the list is fuzzy, it is still living in people's heads.

**Tie each one to the record that should create it.** The closed job, the signed contract, the delivered order. The next event comes from that record, with the last scope still attached.

**Name an owner and a lead time.** One person. The task appears early enough to act. Name who covers when that person is out. The team is not an owner.

**Write the exception.** No answer. Bad contact. Date already past. They want different work this time. Each one gets a state and a next step. Silence is not a status.

**Decide what an agent may draft and what a person has to send.** The draft can be waiting. The relationship stays with someone you can name on a Tuesday.

New-customer work will keep getting the budget. The revenue you already earned will not show up in that meeting. It stays yours only if the close of this job is what creates the start of the next one.
