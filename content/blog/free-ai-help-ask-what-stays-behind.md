On Monday, OpenAI launched OpenWorks, a pilot that pays local AI experts to work with small businesses in person, at no cost to the business. [OpenAI announced it on X](https://x.com/OpenAI/status/2124922133545714160) as a way to "help small businesses develop the tools and skills they need to grow their own business."

The details, from [OpenAI's program page](https://openai.com/openworks/) and [help center](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001724-how-to-apply-to-openworks-and-follow-up-on-your-application):

- Partners are contractors who commit at least four full days a month to local businesses plus one community workshop, for a stipend of up to $8,000 a month.
- Businesses pay nothing and get three months of ChatGPT Business at no cost.
- It is open to independently owned U.S. small businesses: restaurants, retailers, service firms, manufacturers. Software companies, venture-backed businesses and private equity-owned businesses are excluded.
- The pilot is live in 20 communities, from Chicago and Fargo to Charlotte, Atlanta, Nashville and New York. Nothing in Florida yet.

The examples OpenAI posted are the kind of work I like to see. A gift shop in Fargo got a custom dashboard tied to its existing inventory tools, a build the owner estimates would have cost around $50,000 to commission. A sign shop cut vendor price checks from hours to 15 to 20 minutes. [Decrypt's write-up](https://decrypt.co/383848/openai-will-pay-8k-preach-ai-gospel-small-businesses) put it well: the point is to build something useful for the restaurant, not to tell the owner AI will change everything.

## Why this matters

Most small business owners I talk to are not short on AI tools. They are short on someone who will sit next to them, look at how the work actually moves, and build one thing that sticks. That is what OpenWorks is paying for. It is also what we learned the hard way at Infraxio. We went from 30 people to seven, and the tools were the easy part. The hard part was writing down how each workflow really runs, where the data lives, and who owns each step.

So if you are in one of the pilot cities, I would apply. Free, hands-on help with a real project is a good deal. But go in with a plan for what happens after the partner's days with you are done and the free months run out.

## What I would ask before day one

**Pick one problem with a number on it.** Not "use AI more." Something like "we lose four hours a week reconciling inventory" or "quotes take three days to go out." If you cannot measure it before, you will not know whether it worked after.

**Know where your data lives.** The Fargo dashboard worked because it connected to inventory tools the shop already used. If your customer list is in three spreadsheets and someone's phone, fix that first or make it part of the project.

**Ask where the thing you build will live.** Is it a ChatGPT project, a custom tool, a script on someone's laptop? Who can open it, edit it and fix it when it breaks?

**Write it down.** One page: what it does, what it connects to, what to do when it gives a wrong answer. The partner will move on to the next business. Your team will still be using it.

**Name an owner inside your company.** Somebody on your team should be in every session, not just you. OpenAI's own page says the goal is skills "you and your team can use independently." Hold them to that.

**Budget for month four.** Three free months of ChatGPT Business is a trial. Decide up front whether the tool is worth paying for once it ends.

This is the same order we follow when we build hubs for clients with IFX Hub: workflow first, data second, tool third. It is why we can stand up a full business hub in under three weeks instead of the four to six months it used to take us. The model matters less than the plan around it.

OpenWorks is a good sign that the industry is figuring out where the gap really is. It is not the software. It is the hours of someone who understands both the business and the tools. If you get those hours for free, spend them on the thing you will still be using a year from now.
