I have seen companies spend six figures on a new inventory management system and still have thirty-percent count discrepancies within six months. The system was not the problem. The process was.

## Where Accuracy Breaks Down

Inventory accuracy breaks down at specific points: receiving, putaway, picking, and adjustment. If the receiving team does not verify quantities against the PO, the count is wrong from the start. If putaway is informal — pallets go wherever there is space — the system says one location and reality says another. If picking is done by memory instead of by scan, the wrong item ships.

## The Discipline of Cycle Counting

Full physical inventory counts are disruptive and inaccurate. By the time you finish counting section Z, section A has changed. Cycle counting — counting a portion of inventory every day — is more accurate and less disruptive. The key is consistency: the count has to happen every day, and discrepancies have to be investigated immediately, not batched for later.

## System-Enforced Process

The right system enforces good process. Requiring a scan at receiving means the quantity is verified before it enters the system. Directed putaway means the system assigns a location and the worker confirms it. Scan-to-pick means the system verifies the item before it goes in the box. Each of these is a checkpoint that prevents an error from propagating.

## The Ninety-Eight Percent Target

World-class inventory accuracy is ninety-eight percent or higher. Getting there is not about buying better software; it is about executing the process consistently, investigating every discrepancy, and treating inventory accuracy as an operational discipline rather than a periodic project.
