There is a version of consulting where someone shows up with a slide deck, tells you what you should do, and leaves before the system goes live. I have been on the receiving end of that, and it is why Infraxio exists.

## Living With the System

When I implement a system for a client, I treat it the same way I treat the systems that run my own companies. That means I am not optimizing for a clean demo; I am optimizing for the Tuesday morning when three things go wrong simultaneously and the person at the keyboard needs to fix all of them without calling anyone.

## Operators Think in Workflows

The difference between an operator and a traditional consultant shows up in how they scope a project. A consultant asks what features you want. An operator asks what your team does between 7 AM and 5 PM and where the friction lives. Features are a means to an end; the end is a workflow that runs without unnecessary manual steps.

## Why This Matters for Adoption

Systems designed by operators get adopted faster because they match the way people already work, with improvements rather than replacements. If the warehouse team currently prints pick tickets, an operator does not rip out the printer on day one. They digitize the pick ticket first, let the team get comfortable, and then introduce mobile scanning in phase two.

## The Standard I Hold

Every system I deliver has to meet three criteria: it runs the business, the client's team adopts it, and the numbers move. If any one of those is missing, the project is not done.
